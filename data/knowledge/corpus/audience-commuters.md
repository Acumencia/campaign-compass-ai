## Research provenance
These are invented qualitative scenarios for an assignment, not real interviews. Six fictional commuter personas were written to explore carrying needs; no participant recruitment, consent records or population inference exists.

## Carrying needs
Four of the six fictional scenarios mention keeping small items accessible during transfers. Two prioritize room for a change of clothes. These counts describe authored scenarios, not measured customer prevalence.

## Weather concerns
Three fictional scenarios ask about rain exposure. Dayline documentation permits only light-splash resistance. A research desire for waterproofing is not evidence that the product is waterproof.

## Messaging hypotheses
Test a hypothesis emphasizing documented capacity and practical organization. Do not claim that this message increases sales; the synthetic research supports a question to investigate, not a causal marketing result.

## Limits
The scenarios omit wheelchair access, long-distance travel and many body types. Ask users about specific needs. Do not infer demographic attributes or universal comfort from this small fictional set.
